# Diskless metadata coordinator v3: map

## Destination

A v3 of the multi-coordinator design, written in this folder as `design.md` and `spec.md`, that closes the findings of two reviews of [v2](../diskless-metadata-coordinator-v2/design.md): the [council review](reviews/council.md) and [Fable's review](reviews/fable.md). The map is done when every finding from both reviews has a decided fix, with a failure-mode row and a named test, or an accepted risk that passes the data-loss bar in Notes, with a signal or an operator procedure. Nothing is left to decide before the rewrite. Re-running both reviews on v3 comes after the map closes.

## Notes

- Base: v2 as written in [../diskless-metadata-coordinator-v2/](../diskless-metadata-coordinator-v2/map.md), with sequence leases, tracking partitions, fences, floors, and releases. v2 is frozen as the reviewed input: read it, never edit it. `docs/inkless/proposals/` is read-only, per its `AGENTS.md`.
- v2 decisions can be rewritten, and interfaces such as `ControlPlane`, `FileCommitter`, and `FileCommitJob` can change when that fits the fix better.
- Data-loss bar, which replaces v2's:
  1. Any failure that a classic topic survives must be survived.
  2. A failure that a classic topic doesn't survive can be an accepted risk only if its damage stays within the user partitions of the failed coordinator partition, and it loses no more than a classic topic loses to the same failure. One exception: losing every replica of a coordinator partition is an accepted risk when replication factor 3 and rack spread are enforced by a guard, which [Enforced replication factor and rack spread](issues/11-replication-guard.md) decides. A footer recovery would meet clause 2 without the exception, and [Object footer and recovery after losing a replica set](issues/09-object-footer.md) records its design as future work. Under the exception, v3 has no procedure that brings the coordinator partition back: it stays leaderless, and about 1/N of every diskless topic stays unavailable. [Empty restart of a coordinator partition](issues/19-empty-restart.md) records that procedure as future work.
  3. A failure that lets one coordinator partition's loss delete or resurrect another partition's data can't be an accepted risk. It needs a fix or a bound. The rows for a lost tail, an apply bug, and an outside delete in [Deletion bounds: soft-delete requirement and a deletion-rate cap](issues/10-deletion-bounds.md#accepted-risks-in-v3) are the exception. They're accepted risks, because a classic topic loses data to the same causes. A soft-delete bound would meet clause 3 without the exception, and that ticket records its design as future work.
- The rewrite defines "Kafka parity" and "blast radius" in the design's Terms section with the meanings in the bar.
- Performance bar: no worse than the Postgres backend, as in v2. v3 makes it testable with benchmark pass criteria. Running the benchmarks stays out of scope. Object-storage requests match Postgres's, because v3 uploads the same objects and [routing changes no PUT count](issues/22-tracking-part-under-gateways.md#working-notes).
- A finding is closed by one of two things: a fix, with a failure-mode row and a named test, or an accepted risk that passes the bar, with a signal or an operator procedure. Fable's five missing tests are the named tests of the tickets that own their findings.
- Domain: [GLOSSARY.md](../../docs/inkless/GLOSSARY.md). Kafka references: look them up in the local Kafka checkout at `/Users/viktor.somogyi-vass/dev/github/apache/kafka`, not on the web.
- Style for the design and spec: [AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md), engineering rules and style section.
- Tracker: local markdown. Tickets live in [issues/](issues/), one file per ticket. A ticket's `Assignee` line is its claim. Research findings live in [research/](research/), because `.scratch/` is ignored by git. The reviews live in [reviews/](reviews/).
- Grilling tickets call the `grilling` and `domain-modeling` skills and ask one question at a time.
- Hint-only produce gateways are in scope as a cost optimization with no change to correctness. A produce that reaches any broker commits as before, and only the number of commit parts per file changes.

## Decisions so far

- [When the tracking partition fences silent participants](issues/01-silent-participant-fences.md): the broker sends a finish report, `finishedThrough` plus per-participant exception ranges, on its tracking parts and lease requests. The tracking partition fences only exceptions, missed sequences, and lease ends, and only after the broker finished the file. On the normal path, it doesn't fence at commit or retention time.

- [WAL object format and footer feasibility](issues/07-wal-object-format.md): a footer is feasible in the same upload, and readers ignore trailing bytes. It can't hold offsets, commit outcomes, or later removals, only what the broker knows before the commit.
- [Fsync and checkpoint cost per commit](issues/12-fsync-checkpoint-cost.md): one file costs up to `N × R` log flushes against one for Postgres, and a commit waits on two in a row. SQLite syncs only at checkpoints and WAL restarts, and one transaction per high-watermark advance is feasible with savepoints.
- [Lease lifecycle at the broker: fallback ranges and idle ends](issues/02-lease-lifecycle.md): a live broker keeps its lease with keepalives at half the idle timeout, switches at once to a range at a new partition, and ends abandoned ranges with a lease release scoped to a range end. Urgent finish reports go out within one commit interval. The idle timer is a backstop for cut-off or paused brokers.
- [Object-storage soft-delete and versioning](issues/08-object-storage-soft-delete.md): S3 versioning with a noncurrent rule, GCS default soft delete, or Azure blob soft delete each keep deletes recoverable for about 7 days at the cost of upload rate times the window. No broker credential can check the setting, but S3 and Azure delete responses report a soft delete.
- [Image edge cases: re-registered broker IDs and unreconciled partitions](issues/03-image-edge-cases.md): broker-epoch-retired carries a `retiredThrough` epoch taken from the image offset, because a broker epoch is its registration offset. Partition-created stores a `metadataOffset` that gates topic-deleted. Calls for an unreconciled partition get `COORDINATOR_LOAD_IN_PROGRESS`, and a new apply rule backs that up.
- [File completion and story 83](issues/04-file-completion.md): each produce request completes once the parts that hold its batches finish, and the tracking part counts only when it holds one of them. A stuck coordinator partition adds no delay to other requests and at most one commit deadline to its own. File completion still drives the finish report and cleanup.
- [Release reports: ordering, batching, and a leak signal](issues/05-release-reports.md): "released" is terminal, and the tracking partition stores a report for an unrecorded sequence. Entries move forward through pending, fencing, held, and released, and the tracking partition skips a fence whose entries are all held or released. A report lists sequence ranges, with one in flight per tracking partition. Gauges on pending and fencing ages and on pending releases show a stuck release.
- [Fence fan-out under correlated failure](issues/06-fence-fan-out.md): each tracking partition keeps one batched fence request in flight per participant, so a recovering partition gets at most `N - 1` at once. `finishedThrough` now covers the whole broker epoch, so a missed-sequence or lease-end fence never fails a part under another tracking partition's range. `FenceRate` gets new types, and `PendingFenceRangeCount` shows the backlog.
- [Enforced replication factor and rack spread](issues/11-replication-guard.md): startup validation requires a replication factor of at least 3, and an internal config lowers it for tests. Replicas get maximal spread over the registered racks, a reassignment that narrows it gets `INVALID_REPLICA_ASSIGNMENT`, and `CoordinatorPartitionsBelowRackSpread` shows spread that decays. The controller creates the topic once enough unfenced brokers register. Losing a replica set is accepted, and the coordinator partition stays leaderless, because the empty restart is future work.
- [SQLite apply and checkpoint off the shard thread](issues/13-sqlite-apply-checkpoint.md): a checkpoint thread for the whole broker runs `PASSIVE` checkpoints at 1000 pages on a second connection, which needs SQLite 3.51.3, 3.44.6, or 3.50.7. One transaction applies each high-watermark advance, with a savepoint per record and a 4 MiB cap. The follower applier gets its own thread, and `SqliteWalPages` and `ApplyLagBytes` show WAL growth and apply lag.
- [Fsync amplification and default N on small clusters](issues/14-small-cluster-fsync.md): the default `N` stays a constant 8, provisional, because the topic is created at about 3 brokers. 3 brokers is the smallest supported cluster and the first benchmark, at `N` of 1, 2, 4, and 8. The append linger defaults to adaptive. Outgrowing `N` is an accepted performance limit, signaled by the runtime and commit metrics.
- [Admission cap or retry budget](issues/15-admission-cap.md): no admission cap or retry budget. In-flight limits per broker cap a leader at about `B` commit parts and `8B` read parts, retries back off exponentially, and the overlay cap is `4 × B` entries with `COORDINATOR_NOT_AVAILABLE`. Reads move to a leader read pool, `diskless.metadata.read.threads`, and an asynchronous `findBatches` with a 1000 ms deadline and 8 slots per coordinator partition.
- [Benchmark pass criteria](issues/16-benchmark-pass-criteria.md): the baseline is Postgres in production configuration, and P50 and P99 must stay within 1.1 times it, median of 3 runs. The benchmarks run at 3, 12, and 24 brokers on 16 MiB/s and 64 partitions per broker, and each has a pass rule, including partial failure, inter-AZ bytes, and a retention burst. A failure retunes a default, blocks release as a defect, or ships the backend as opt-in with Postgres the default. [laptop-bench/](laptop-bench/README.md) gauges the main risks in Docker.
- [Routing produce by coordinator partition](issues/20-gateway-routing.md): the transformer must hash v2's coordinator mapping, not `"<topicId>-<partitionIndex>"`, and the mapping and assignment fit in `SharedState`. Fetches move with produce and keep AZ alignment. Clients without `diskless_az` already get a rewritten `LeaderId`, so they can be routed too. Remote-storage topics stay on replica-only routing unless placement aligns, and a live old gateway keeps traffic for up to `metadata.max.age.ms`.
- [Gateway assignment](issues/21-gateway-assignment.md): every broker computes rendezvous hashing with a cap, per rack, from unfenced brokers that aren't excluded by a per-broker config, with `max(1, ⌊b_r / N⌋)` gateways per coordinator partition and leadership not an input. A fallback order keeps clients in their AZ, and a fixed-value test pins the rule. The busiest gateway carries up to 1.7 times today's busiest broker at 15 per rack. Meters count routed and misrouted commit parts and misrouted bytes.
- [Tracking part under gateways](issues/22-tracking-part-under-gateways.md): a broker's tracking partition is one of its gateway coordinator partitions, chosen by a sticky rule behind an interface that also orders the fallbacks, so a routed file sends 1 part instead of 1.88 at saturation. A broker switches at the first file with no batch for its old tracking partition, with renewal reason `assignment`. The commit-parts meter counts empty tracking parts, and the model needs no new work.
- [Routing policy for clients a gateway can't serve](issues/24-gateway-routing-policy.md): every diskless partition routes to gateways except one whose fetches need a broker-local log, which in Inkless v3 means consolidating, tiered, and phase-1 switched partitions. Unknown-AZ clients count as the rack-less group, and the replica preference goes. A broker that isn't a gateway rejects a misrouted batch with `NOT_LEADER_OR_FOLLOWER` once per connection and partition per `metadata.max.age.ms`, then commits. A cut-off fenced gateway is a cost-only accepted risk, and routing turns on only with the feature, the topic, and a cluster-level switch in the image.
- [Gateway benchmark](issues/23-gateway-benchmark.md): gateways cut parts per file at every size from 2 brokers per rack up, so `diskless.gateway.routing.enable` is on by default with no threshold and settable only at the cluster default. Pairs against gateways off run at 12, 21, 24, 45, and 48 brokers, with a decision rule that turns the default off only if latency fails where load is even.

- [Model of the fence, floor, and release protocol](issues/17-protocol-model.md): three TLA+ specs reproduce every reviewed race against the old rules and find no violation in v3 within their bounds. A lost tail damages only its own partition. The model adds three fixes and two batching rules to earlier tickets, and a test pins the controller behavior that the `retiredThrough` rule depends on.
- [Consequences for the cross-cutting sections](issues/18-cross-cutting-consequences.md): every finding closes and every accepted risk passes the bar. Failure tables get v2's three columns and a Gateway routing group, six derived rows close the findings that lacked one, performance limits move to Accepted limits, metrics join the groups that record them with four new names, a coordinator-partition fault hook carries the lost-tail suite, stories 97 to 105 join, and Gateway routing becomes a design section.

## Out of scope

- Running the benchmarks. v3 defines their pass criteria only.
- An off-broker copy of coordinator state and a restore tool.
- [Object footer and recovery after losing a replica set](issues/09-object-footer.md): a future development idea. The ticket records the full design: a footer of partition runs with offset hints, and an operator-triggered recovery that lists the bucket, filters runs by the surviving tracking partitions, and serves the records at new offsets above a floor. v3 relaxes clause 2 for this case instead, and the rewrite records the design in Future work.
- Recovery from losing the KRaft metadata. Classic topics don't survive it either, so clause 2 holds without coordinator work.
- [Deletion bounds: soft-delete requirement and a deletion-rate cap](issues/10-deletion-bounds.md): a future development idea. The ticket records the full design: recoverable deletes kept for at least 7 days, checked by an hourly probe and each delete response, read-time detection with an operator restore, and a deletion-rate cap relative to each tracking partition's upload rate. v3 relaxes clause 3 for the rows for a lost tail, an apply bug, and an outside delete instead, and the rewrite records the design in Future work.
- [Empty restart of a coordinator partition](issues/19-empty-restart.md): a future development idea, deferred because whether it's needed is still debated. The ticket records the full design: an `UNCLEAN` election accepted only once the ISR, ELR, and `lastKnownElr` are empty, a durable restart epoch that empties every replica's log, participant-restarted fences at the other tracking partitions, a per-broker floor fence with a gate, abandoned ranges released over their full span, lazy adoption of released objects, and a restart above the seal and the remote tier. No v3 component depends on it, and the rewrite records it in Future work.
- A replay checker and an inspect-and-repair tool for a deterministic apply bug. v2's signal still detects the bug's effect, and the deferred soft-delete bound would make its deletes recoverable.
- The single-partition direction of the earlier effort.
- Changing the partition count of `__diskless_metadata` after creation, and moving an object's tracking partition after the commit, as in v2.
